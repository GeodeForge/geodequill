The Chinese machine-writing list in scripts/voice_check.py follows the categories of
Humanizer-zh (https://github.com/op7418/Humanizer-zh), and the Spanish list follows the
Spanish rule pack of Signs of AI Writing (https://github.com/peopleworks/SignsofAI).
Both are used under their MIT licences, reproduced below.

The Spanish common-word list that scripts/voice_check.py uses to tell languages apart is
taken from the Snowball Spanish stop-word list (https://snowballstem.org), used under the
3-clause BSD licence reproduced below.

The table that matches traditional Chinese characters was generated with OpenCC
(https://github.com/BYVoid/OpenCC, Apache License 2.0); no OpenCC code or data file ships here.

The Japanese, Korean, German, Russian, Turkish and Finnish machine-writing lists in
scripts/voice_check.py were drawn from these published lists, then cut down on human writing
dated before November 2022 (see the product's measurement notes). Each is used under its MIT
licence, reproduced below:
  Japanese: humanizer-ja (https://github.com/gonta223/humanizer-ja) and patina
            (https://github.com/devswha/patina)
  Korean:   patina and humanizer-ko (https://github.com/choconyam/humanizer-ko)
  German:   humanizer-de (https://github.com/marmbiz/humanizer-de). Some of its catalogue is
            adapted from the German and English Wikipedia pages on signs of AI-generated text
            (CC BY-SA 4.0); the German list here takes short phrases from it, credited here.
  Russian:  humanizer-ru (https://github.com/Vladimir-Human/humanizer-ru) and humanizer-ru
            (https://github.com/thevseprod/humanizer-ru)
  Turkish:  turkce-humanizer (https://github.com/bushrabeg/turkce-humanizer)
