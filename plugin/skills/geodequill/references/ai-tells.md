# Machine-writing habits to remove

Based on the public catalog "Signs of AI writing" kept by Wikipedia's WikiProject AI Cleanup, reworded and trimmed. The script catches the mechanical ones. Judgment catches the rest.

## Words and phrases

- **Stock vocabulary:** delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, vibrant, intricate, underscore, showcase, foster, garner, multifaceted, leverage, robust, seamless, elevate, holistic, transformative. Use the plain word instead.
- **Stock phrases:** "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note", "plays a crucial role", "navigate the complexities", "unlock the potential", "shed light on", "at the end of the day", "when it comes to".
- **Chat leftovers:** "I hope this helps", "let me know if", "great question", "certainly!", "feel free to".
- **Grand significance:** calling something a game-changer, a turning point, or a lasting legacy when nothing in the text earns it.
- **Fancy stand-ins for "is" and "has":** serves as, stands as, boasts, features. Write "is" and "has".

## Sentence shapes

- **Negative parallelism:** "It's not just X, it's Y." Say Y.
- **Reflex triples:** three adjectives or three items every time. Use the number the content has.
- **Tacked-on -ing clause:** ", highlighting the importance of...", ", ensuring that...". Cut it, or make it its own sentence with a real claim.
- **Vague attribution:** "experts say", "studies show". In a draft, name the source if the writer gave one, otherwise drop the claim. In a rewrite, keep it as written and flag it in the closing line.
- **Stacked hedges:** "may potentially", "could possibly". Pick one hedge or none.
- **Question then answer:** "The result? Chaos." "Here's the thing:". State it.
- **Synonym cycling:** calling the same thing by a new name each sentence to avoid repeating a word. Repeat the word.
- **False ranges:** "from X to Y" where there is no real scale between them.

## Structure and layout

- **Wrap-up lines:** a closing sentence on every paragraph that restates it, and a final "Overall, ..." paragraph. End when the point is made.
- **Every-side balance:** "While X, it's also important to consider Y" used on every point.
- **Even rhythm:** all sentences about the same length, all paragraphs about the same size.
- **Formatting for show:** Title Case headings, a header over every short section, bold-lead bullets ("**Key point:** ..."), emoji in headings, bullets where two sentences would do.
- **Sycophantic opener:** praising the question or the idea before answering.
- **Connector pile-up:** Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally at the start of consecutive sentences.

## In other languages

The habits above have counterparts in other languages. The checker's Chinese list takes its categories from Humanizer-zh, a Chinese edition of the same Wikipedia catalogue, and adds phrases Chinese readers named in 2026, checked against human Chinese writing; its Spanish list is adapted from the Spanish rules of Signs of AI Writing (PeopleWorks). The Japanese, Korean, Turkish, German, Russian and Finnish lists come from published humanizer projects in those languages. All are MIT-licensed; their notices are in `LICENSE.txt` in this skill. Every list was trimmed on real human writing before it shipped, and a lot was cut: in Turkish, semicolons, dashes and "-mektedir" turned out to be ordinary written Turkish, and in Russian "является" and "не только …, но и" are everyday words, so none of them count.

- **Chinese.** Chat leftovers (希望这对你有帮助, 如果您还有其他问题); grand openers (在当今社会, 随着…的不断发展, 在…的时代背景下); inflated significance (彰显了, 标志着…新时代, 发挥着至关重要的作用); 不仅仅是…更是…; vague experts (一些专家认为); stacked hedges (也许可能); chat-style comfort and offers (稳稳地接住, 一句话总结, 如果你愿意，我可以); 不是…而是… repeated through a piece; and stock words such as 赋能, 至关重要, 深入探讨, 助力, 无缝. The checker also counts the —— dash: more than an occasional one is a habit worth breaking. 总而言之 and 综上所述 count only when they pile up with 此外 and 值得注意的是, because human writers use them too.
- **Spanish.** Chat leftovers (espero que esto te ayude, no dudes en); grand openers (en la era digital); filler (cabe destacar que, es importante señalar, vale la pena mencionar); inflated phrases (juega un papel crucial, se erige como); "no se trata solo de"; tacked-on gerunds (", destacando", ", subrayando"); vague experts (los expertos afirman); stacked hedges (podría potencialmente).
- **Japanese.** Chat leftovers (お役に立てれば幸いです, ご不明な点がございましたら, 素晴らしいご質問ですね); stock evaluations (浮き彫りにしており, と言えるでしょう, 重要な役割を果たしています); template closers (今後の展開が注目されます, さらなる発展が期待されます); 単なる…ではなく; vague experts (専門家によると). することができます is ordinary in Japanese technical writing and is not counted.
- **Korean.** Chat leftovers (도움이 되셨기를 바랍니다, 궁금한 점이 있으시면, 물론입니다); stock closers (귀추가 주목됩니다, 밝은 미래가 기대됩니다); inflated phrases (새로운 지평을 열다, 토대를 마련하다, 패러다임의 전환); 단순히 …를 넘어; double passives (되어지다, 보여지다); vague experts (전문가들은).
- **Turkish.** Inflated phrases (kritik bir rol oynamaktadır, hayati bir önem taşımaktadır, vazgeçilmez bir hale gelmiştir); "değil, aynı zamanda" and "değil, sadece"; staged questions (şu soru gündemde). Connectors such as bu bağlamda and bunun yanı sıra count only in piles.
- **German.** Chat leftovers (ich hoffe, das hilft; lassen Sie mich wissen); filler (es ist wichtig zu beachten, es ist erwähnenswert); grand openers (in der heutigen digitalen Welt, im Zeitalter der Digitalisierung); "es geht nicht um … – es geht um"; spielt eine entscheidende Rolle; stock words such as nahtlos, vielschichtig, maßgeschneidert. "nicht nur … sondern auch" and "außerdem" are ordinary German and are not counted.
- **Russian.** Chat leftovers (надеюсь, это поможет; если хотите, могу); filler (стоит отметить, важно понимать); grand openers (в современном мире); inflated phrases (ключевую роль, раскрыть потенциал, широкий спектр); "не просто …, а"; vague experts (эксперты отмечают). The checker reads ё as е.
- **Finnish.** Over-polite English calques (olisin erittäin kiitollinen, voisitte ystävällisesti); filler (on syytä huomata, tässä yhteydessä on tärkeää); vague experts (asiantuntijoiden mukaan, tutkimukset osoittavat); empty optimism (tulevaisuus näyttää valoisalta, avaa uusia mahdollisuuksia); doubled significance (merkittävä ja keskeinen).
- **Every language:** even rhythm, formatting for show, emoji and bold-lead bullets read the same way in any script. (Thai, Lao and Tibetan do not mark where sentences end, so there only paragraph rhythm is measured.)

## How strong each signal is

Measured on three collections of human writing: 23 books by Mark Twain (1.74 million words), 85,000 words of his speeches, and 141 documents that ship with ordinary software packages (192,000 words). No collection of machine-written text has been measured yet. Measured later on modern English, the same phrases fired more often: about 1.6 per 10,000 words in mixed web and news text, and far more in office email: 16 of 38 files of human office email had at least one, almost all "let me know if". The Chinese and Spanish lists were measured on books (Chinese 1898 to 1971, Spanish novels 1880 to 1939), 1998 Chinese news, 2013 Chinese shopping reviews and the translated Python documentation: 0.2 strong tells per 10,000 words or fewer in each. Modern Spanish web text ran higher, about 0.6. The Japanese, Korean, Turkish, German, Russian and Finnish lists were measured on novels and stories written before 1950, the translated Python documentation and Pro Git, and Wikipedia articles as they stood before November 2022: 0.3 per 10,000 words or fewer in every collection of 100,000 words or more. None of them has been measured on office email or web text.

- **Phrases and patterns are strong.** They fired 0.2 to 0.3 times per 10,000 words in all three collections. Fix every one.
- **A single stock word is weak.** 0.6 and 1.4 per 10,000 words in the books and the software documents (intricate, testament, robust, foster, seamlessly turned up). Swap it for a plainer word if one fits. Do not hunt for it.
- **Formatting marks are weak.** Title Case headings, bold labels on bullets, and emoji fired 17 times per 10,000 words in the software documents, all written by people. Remove them from personal prose; in a README they may be the writer's habit.
- **Punctuation and contractions are the writer's habit, not machine marks.** Twain averaged about 9 em dashes and 11 semicolons per 1,000 words. Almost half the software documents used no contractions at all. Match the writer, not a rule.
- **Even rhythm is a hint.** In every one of the 23 books, sentence-length variation stayed between 0.69 and 1.15 and paragraph-size variation between 0.68 and 1.55. One software document in 141 fell below 0.5. Technical writing trips it more often in every language: in the Python documentation, 6% of documents fell below 0.5 in English, 7% in Chinese and 8% in Spanish, and 18% of Chinese technical books and newsletters did. Novels and essays almost never did. Only human text was measured, so low variation is a hint, not proof. These are whole-document figures. Draft-sized stretches of human writing (500 words, or 750 Chinese characters) fall below 0.5 far more often: about 6% of stretches of Spanish novels, 15% to 25% of documentation, news and Chinese books, and 28% to 40% of short shopping reviews. On a short draft, treat it as a reason to look, never as a verdict.

## Check yourself

After removing these, read the text once more. If it is clean but nobody seems to be behind it, add voice using only what the writer gave you: their opinion, a real detail, a plain remark. If they gave none, ask. See `human-texture.md`.
