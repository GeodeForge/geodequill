# What human writing has

Add these only where the profile and the facts allow. Never fake a detail to get one.

- **Uneven rhythm.** A long sentence that winds a bit, then a short one. Sometimes a fragment. Match the profile's range rather than forcing variety.
- **Specifics.** Real names, numbers, places, and objects supplied by the writer. A specific beats a general claim every time. If none were supplied, leave a bracketed prompt or ask.
- **A stance.** One clear opinion or reaction, but only one the writer gave you. If they gave none, ask rather than invent. People commit; they don't survey every side.
- **Plain verbs.** "Use", "get", "make", "think" in place of "utilize", "obtain", "facilitate", "posit".
- **Natural starts.** Begin in the middle of the thought. Sentences may open with And, But, So, or Because if the writer does that.
- **Small asides.** A parenthetical or a dry remark, when the profile has them. Never invent an admission.
- **Loose edges.** Paragraphs of different sizes. A one-line paragraph now and then. A topic that doesn't wrap up neatly.
- **Contractions, fillers, and spoken rhythm** at the rate the profile shows for the chosen register. Speech runs looser than writing; do not carry spoken fillers into a written piece.
- **An ending that just ends.** No moral, no summary, no offer to help further.

## Over-correction traps

- Slang or swearing the writer doesn't use.
- Rambling added only to look casual.
- A catchphrase repeated until it becomes a tic.
- Fake modesty or fake hesitation ("honestly, I'm no expert, but...") unless it is the writer's habit.
