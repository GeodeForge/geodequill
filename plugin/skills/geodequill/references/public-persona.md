# Voices of real, named people

## 1. Decide whether you may search

- **Public-domain author.** The person's works are in the public domain where the user is. Rule of thumb for the United States as of 2026: works published in 1930 or earlier. Rules differ by country. You may search for the person's written and spoken work. Go to section 2.
- **Living or recently dead public figure.** Do not search for their work. Their articles, books, and interviews are copyrighted. The user may supply samples they have the right to use. The profile keeps measurements and at most five short phrases per register, never passages. Go to section 4.
- **Private person.** Do not build their voice from material gathered about them. Their own writing, supplied by them, is fine.
- **Unsure.** Treat the person as not public domain.

## 2. Find sources (public-domain authors)

Search for both registers:

- **Written:** collected works on public archives: Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, Internet Archive, HathiTrust.
- **Spoken:** collected speeches, lecture texts, verbatim interview transcripts, recorded talks with transcripts. A dated list of the person's talks or interviews helps find them.

Tag every spoken source with its reliability:

- **Verbatim:** the person's own speech text, a stenographic record, or a transcript of a recording. Measure it.
- **Reported:** a reporter's write-up of what was said. Old newspaper interviews are usually this. It may inform the personality notes, but do not measure it.
- **Doubtful:** quote collections and sayings attributed without a source. Do not use.

The person's own books count as written, even where they contain dialogue. Measure narration or essays, not invented characters' speech.

## 3. Get the text

The web reader returns summaries, not exact text, and many archives refuse it. Summaries cannot be measured. So:

1. List up to six sources with links, each marked written or spoken and with its reliability.
2. Ask the user to download plain-text files and attach them. A folder of files works.
3. Ask for at least 600 words per register. More is better; whole books are fine.

Do not measure text pieced together from search-result snippets. Snippets are partial and sometimes altered.

## 4. Writing as a real person

- Present output as "in the style of <name>". Never present it as written or said by the person.
- Never put an invented quote in the person's mouth.
- Personality notes for a real person come from their own work, not from the model's general impression of them. Say where each note came from.
