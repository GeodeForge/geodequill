# Tones

A tone changes how the voice speaks this time. It never changes who is speaking: keep the voice's vocabulary, rhythm, signature habits and personality, and shift only what the tone names. The rules in `SKILL.md` still apply in every tone. A persuasive tone does not license an invented statistic; an intimate tone does not license an invented shared memory.

**Strength.** "Slightly formal" is a slight shift, "very formal" a strong one; with no word, use a clear shift. A slight shift keeps most of the voice's own habits; a strong one follows the tone's list closely.

**Custom tones.** A user can describe a tone of their own and ask to save it. Save it as `<name>.md` in the `tones` folder beside the personal voices folder (`.geodequill/tones` in the home folder), with the same three parts as below: an "In one line:" line, a "Moves:" line, and a few lines of guidance.

Each tone's "Moves:" line names the measures the tone pushes up or down. The checker uses it to stop flagging those shifts as off-voice. Measures: contractions, fillers, exclamations, questions, first person, sentence length, dashes, semicolons, reading level.

**Easier to read.** A request to make a text "easier to read", "simpler", "plainer", or to use "less jargon" asks for simpler language than the text already has. Use the plain tone below, at the strength the user asked for.

## professional
In one line: Clear, courteous and businesslike, for colleagues, clients and anything work-related.
Moves: fillers down, exclamations down
- Lead with the point. Keep sentences plain and complete.
- Contractions are fine if the voice uses them; slang, emoji and in-jokes are not.
- Warm but not chummy: thank, acknowledge, move on.

## formal
In one line: Correct, impersonal and careful, for official letters, applications and readers in authority.
Moves: contractions down, fillers down, exclamations down, first person down, sentence length up
- No contractions, no slang, no fillers.
- Full sentences with complete grammar; longer sentences are fine.
- Address the reader by title; keep personal asides out.

## casual
In one line: Relaxed and loose, as the voice talks to friends.
Moves: contractions up, fillers up, sentence length down, exclamations up
- Contractions throughout. Short sentences and fragments are fine.
- The voice's own slang and fillers are welcome; do not add ones it never uses.

## friendly
In one line: Warm and approachable, glad to hear from the reader.
Moves: contractions up, exclamations up, questions up
- Greet the person, show interest, ask after them where it fits.
- Keep it light; one exclamation mark is plenty.

## modest
In one line: Understated and self-effacing; lets the work speak and credits others.
Moves: first person down, exclamations down
- Fewer sentences starting with "I". Say "we" where others helped.
- Report results plainly, without superlatives. Do not undersell facts into falsehood.

## confident
In one line: Sure and direct; states positions without hedging.
Moves: sentence length down, questions down
- Cut hedges ("I think", "maybe", "just") unless the doubt is real.
- Short declarative sentences. Recommend; do not survey.

## intimate
In one line: Close and personal, as to a partner, family member or old friend.
Moves: first person up, contractions up, questions up
- Speak to "you" directly. Plain feeling, said simply.
- Use only shared details the user supplied; never invent a memory.

## playful
In one line: Light and teasing, with room for the voice's own humour.
Moves: exclamations up, questions up, sentence length down
- Humour of the voice's kind only; do not bolt on jokes it would not make.
- Keep the substance intact under the fun.

## direct
In one line: Blunt and brief; the shortest honest way to say it.
Moves: sentence length down, fillers down, questions down
- One idea per sentence. No softeners, no preamble.
- Blunt is not rude: no insults, no sarcasm the voice would not use.

## diplomatic
In one line: Tactful and balanced, for disagreement, feedback or sensitive subjects.
Moves: sentence length up, exclamations down
- Acknowledge the other view before stating your own. Criticise the work, not the person.
- Soften the delivery, never the facts.

## empathetic
In one line: Gentle and understanding, for someone having a hard time.
Moves: questions up, exclamations down, sentence length down
- Acknowledge what the reader is going through before anything else.
- Offer, do not instruct. No silver linings unless the user asks for them.

## persuasive
In one line: Makes a case and asks for something, for pitches, appeals and proposals.
Moves: questions up, sentence length down
- Lead with what the reader gains. End with one clear request.
- Use only the user's real evidence; no invented numbers, quotes or testimonials.

## plain
In one line: Easier to read: everyday words and shorter sentences, with every fact kept.
Moves: sentence length down, reading level down
- Prefer the everyday word to the long or technical one. Explain a term the reader needs; replace one they do not.
- Break long sentences up. One idea per sentence.
- Keep every fact, number, name, date and caveat. Plainer is not shorter by dropping things.
- It is still the same voice: keep its personality and attitude, and say it more simply.
